# Philoo Dóxa Progressive Discovery

## Goal

Turn the Dóxa scene from a simultaneous collection of cards into a short guided discovery. The student should perform one mental action at a time: learn the word, observe incomplete evidence, reveal the missing context, understand the mistake, and connect it to Plato's cave.

## Experience

The scene uses one central stage with four progressive moments. It is not a carousel of unrelated slides: each moment replaces the previous one inside the same visual frame and preserves the student's place in the story.

### 1. Meaning

- Show `δόξα` and the concise definition: an opinion or belief formed from how something appears to us.
- Keep the image and Plato hidden.
- The primary action is `Ver um exemplo`.

### 2. Observe

- Show the cropped crayon image as the dominant element.
- Ask: `Olhando apenas para este recorte, o que parece estar acontecendo?`
- Do not ask for a graded answer or assume what the student thought.
- The image receives the looping tap cue. Clicking anywhere on it advances the discovery.

### 3. Reveal

- Expand the same image to show both children and the complete context.
- Explain: `Talvez tenha parecido que as crianças disputavam o giz. Era uma conclusão possível com a parte que você tinha. Agora vemos que uma criança estava entregando o giz à outra.`
- Make clear that the first impression was reasonable, not a failure.
- The primary action is `Entender o que aconteceu`.

### 4. Connect

- Plato appears only at this moment, large and aligned with his speech.
- Plato explains: `Isso é dóxa: formar uma crença a partir do que conseguimos perceber. Os prisioneiros faziam o mesmo com as sombras. Eles não estavam inventando — tentavam compreender o mundo usando apenas uma parte dele.`
- The primary action continues to the next lesson moment.

## Interaction and Motion

- Transitions use a short horizontal dissolve and gentle scale change; content should feel like the same thought unfolding.
- The tap cue loops until the image is clicked, then disappears for the rest of the mounted scene.
- Completed moment indicators remain subtle and do not compete with the current content.
- Reduced-motion users receive an immediate opacity transition with no looping gesture.
- Browser back/forward behavior remains unchanged because the four moments belong to one lesson route.

## Visual Direction

- Preserve Philoo's cream, baby-blue, rounded, tactile identity.
- Use one central content surface rather than separate definition, Plato, and image columns.
- The active content should occupy the available stage confidently on desktop while remaining a single vertical composition on tablet and phone.
- Keep typography, shadows, button depth, and border treatment consistent with the established lesson template.
- Spend visual emphasis on the image reveal; avoid decorative elements that do not teach.

## Accessibility

- Every stage has a descriptive heading.
- The full image button remains keyboard accessible.
- The tap cue is decorative and hidden from assistive technology.
- Focus moves to the new stage heading after explicit button-driven transitions.
- The image alt text changes with the revealed context.

## Verification

- Test the four-stage sequence and exact pedagogical copy.
- Verify that Plato is absent before the final connection.
- Verify that the tap cue disappears after the first reveal.
- Check desktop, tablet, and phone layouts.
- Confirm reduced-motion behavior and keyboard activation.
