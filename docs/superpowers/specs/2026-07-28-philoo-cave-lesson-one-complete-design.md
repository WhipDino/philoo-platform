# Philoo Cave — Lesson One Complete Experience

**Date:** 2026-07-28  
**Status:** Approved learning direction; written specification awaiting review  
**Scope:** Complete the first Cave lesson, from the invitation through the
prisoner's first turn.  
**Primary audience:** Learners approximately 11–16 years old, using desktop,
tablet, or phone independently or with a teacher.

## Product goal

Create a 12–15 minute philosophical story that learners want to continue,
while ensuring they can explain:

1. why the prisoners treat shadows as reality;
2. the difference between what was perceived, inferred, and not yet knowable;
3. how an incomplete point of view can produce sincere but limited beliefs;
4. the child-friendly meaning of **dóxa**;
5. why questioning the wall is the beginning of education.

The experience must feel like a Philoo story rather than a sequence of school
slides. Platão is the guide and an active character. Interaction supports the
story; it never exists only to keep the learner clicking.

## Pedagogical position

Philoo does not design around a fixed, universal child "attention span."
Instead, the lesson changes the learner's mental role at deliberate intervals.
Every one to two minutes, the learner should meaningfully do one of the
following:

- notice;
- predict;
- manipulate;
- retrieve;
- explain;
- decide;
- reflect.

These moments are not all tests. The episode contains:

- short narrative and explanation beats;
- two lightweight participation interactions;
- two scaffolded learning activities;
- one personal reflection;
- one explicitly named philosophical concept.

The lesson provides instruction and a worked example before assessing the same
idea. Feedback explains the reasoning and never removes points, lives, or
status.

## Narrative boundary

This is the first part of a trilogy:

1. **Inside the cave:** the prisoners' world, the mechanism of the shadows,
   dóxa, the first contradiction, and the beginning of the turn.
2. **Leaving the cave:** release, pain, ascent, adjustment to light, and a
   larger account of reality.
3. **Returning:** return to the cave, rejection, responsibility, education,
   and citizenship.

Lesson One must not show the outside world. It ends precisely when the learner
wants to see it.

## Experience rhythm

The lesson uses nine visible journey stages. A stage may contain several short
dialogue beats without adding another item to the journey rail.

| Stage | Purpose | Learner role | Estimated time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. O começo da história | Meet Platão and hear about the cave | Enter | 45–60 s |
| 2. A descida | Leave the outside light and follow Platão | Accompany | 45–60 s |
| 3. Quem vive aqui | Understand the prisoners and their physical limitation | Observe | 60–75 s |
| 4. O jogo da parede | Experience why fast shadow recognition feels like wisdom | Participate | 75–100 s |
| 5. O que existe atrás | Reveal fire, carriers, objects, echoes, and projection | Discover | 75–100 s |
| 6. O caminho da sombra | Reconstruct the mechanism | Build | 90–120 s |
| 7. Uma palavra da filosofia | Learn and apply dóxa | Name the idea | 60–90 s |
| 8. O que chegou até eles | Separate observation, inference, and uncertainty | Reason | 120–180 s |
| 9. A primeira dúvida | Encounter a contradiction and choose whether to turn | Question | 90–120 s |

These durations are design targets, not timers. No learner is rushed.

## Detailed stage design

### Stage 1 — O começo da história

Preserve the approved invitation scene and authentic source mark:

`Platão · A República, Livro VII`

Platão introduces people who have lived deep in a cave since childhood and can
only face a wall. The scene's job is curiosity, not assessment.

### Stage 2 — A descida

Preserve the approved descent. Platão guides the learner away from the
entrance. The transition visually continues the same journey rather than
behaving like a new slide deck.

Participation action: the learner advances the light/path once. It carries no
score and does not interrupt the narration.

### Stage 3 — Quem vive aqui

Preserve the approved prisoner explanation, but ensure it explicitly conveys:

- they have been restrained since childhood;
- they cannot turn their bodies or heads;
- the wall is not merely preferred—it is the only view they know.

The scene avoids language suggesting that the prisoners voluntarily refuse to
look elsewhere.

### Stage 4 — O jogo da parede

Keep the approved story in which prisoners name recurring shadows and celebrate
the fastest recognizer.

Add a short participation interaction named **Jogue como eles**:

1. three familiar shadows cross the wall one at a time;
2. the learner taps the matching name;
3. the response is immediate and celebratory;
4. there is no failure state or score;
5. the final beat lets the learner feel competent in the prisoners' game.

Platão then interrupts the victory with the key question:

> Você reconheceu a sombra. Mas isso significa que sabe o que a produziu?

This question is reflective, not graded.

### Stage 5 — O que existe atrás

The learner discovers the mechanism in three controlled reveals:

1. a fire;
2. people carrying objects between the fire and the prisoners;
3. shadows and echoed voices arriving at the wall.

The reveal uses three large tactile controls. Each control uncovers one
reserved visual layer and advances the causal sequence. It must not depend on
freehand manipulation of an AI-generated background.

Platão is posed toward the currently revealed evidence. Copy is brief and
spatially associated with the relevant element.

### Stage 6 — O caminho da sombra

This is the first scaffolded learning activity.

The learner constructs:

`luz → objeto → sombra → nome`

#### Instruction dialog

Before the activity begins, display a Philoo instructional dialog:

- canonical Platão bust emerging from the lower edge;
- one-sentence purpose;
- a two-step animated or illustrated demonstration;
- explicit input instructions for the current device;
- primary action: **Vamos montar**;
- a persistent **Como jogar** control reopens the dialog.

The dialog is not shown for narrative-only scenes.

#### Mechanics

- Desktop/tablet: drag cards into a horizontal causal path.
- Phone: tap a card, then tap the next open position.
- Keyboard: select and place through ordinary buttons.
- Platão places or demonstrates the first relationship.
- The remaining steps can be rearranged without penalty.
- On completion, the path animates once from light to name.
- Reduced-motion mode replaces movement with sequential highlighting.

Feedback explains cause:

> A sombra precisa de algo entre a luz e a parede.

It never says only "errado."

### Stage 7 — Uma palavra da filosofia

Introduce one authentic concept:

## Dóxa — δόξα

Child-friendly explanation:

> Uma opinião ou crença formada a partir de como algo aparece para nós.

The concept presentation contains:

1. the word and Greek form;
2. a short spoken explanation from Platão;
3. the cave example;
4. one current-life example;
5. one low-stakes application.

The current-life example uses a cropped image that appears to show a complete
event but omits what happened outside the frame.

Do not teach `epistéme` in depth here. It becomes more meaningful after the
ascent in Lesson Two.

### Stage 8 — O que chegou até eles

Move the existing discovery-table activity to this point and keep its approved
cartoon tactile identity.

The learning categories remain:

- **Vi**
- **Concluí**
- **Ainda não sei**

Changes from the current version:

1. Platão models the first card before independent work.
2. The independent set contains four cards, not six.
3. Every card refers directly to an event the learner has seen.
4. Wording avoids ambiguous claims that require knowledge not yet taught.
5. Feedback explains whether the prisoner perceived the claim, supplied it as
   an explanation, or lacked enough evidence.
6. Correct placement produces restrained competence feedback, not points or
   confetti.

Example set:

- `Uma forma atravessou a parede.` → Vi
- `A forma era produzida por um objeto.` → Concluí
- `A voz vinha da própria sombra.` → Concluí
- `Do ponto de vista dos prisioneiros, existia um mundo fora da caverna.` →
  Ainda não sei

The activity heading and instructions explicitly say that every classification
uses the prisoners' point of view.

#### Instruction dialog

Use the same reusable activity-introduction primitive as Stage 6, with
activity-specific content:

- purpose: distinguish what appeared from what was added by thought;
- demonstration: Platão moves the worked-example card;
- desktop instruction: drag to a pocket;
- touch instruction: tap a card, then a pocket;
- **Como jogar** remains available.

### Stage 9 — A primeira dúvida

Introduce an event that does not fit the prisoners' existing expectations. It
must be visually and conceptually understandable without audio.

One prisoner notices the inconsistency. Platão asks what such a contradiction
should make us do.

The learner chooses or writes a short response to:

> Se tudo o que você conhecesse estivesse nesta parede, o que faria você
> desconfiar dela?

Prepared options support younger learners; an optional short text response
supports older learners. No response is marked wrong.

Platão responds by connecting doubt with investigation. The prisoner's
restraint loosens or an invitation to turn appears. The final action is:

**Quero ver além da parede**

The lesson ends before the prisoner sees the fire or exit from their new
orientation. Lesson Two owns the pain and consequences of turning.

## Reusable activity-introduction dialog

The dialog becomes a reusable lesson primitive only after both Lesson One
activities validate it.

Required behavior:

- implemented with an accessible modal dialog;
- focus moves inside on open and returns to the invoking control on close;
- Escape closes when closing is allowed;
- background interaction is blocked;
- no surprise audio;
- clear title, purpose, demonstration, and input instructions;
- input copy adapts to pointer/touch without making device assumptions;
- never reopens automatically after the learner has dismissed it during the
  same activity visit;
- always reopenable through **Como jogar**.

Visual language:

- cream-white paper surface;
- Philoo baby-blue edge and offset lower layer;
- rounded tactile primary action;
- Platão visibly grounded in or emerging from the dialog edge;
- no generic corporate onboarding illustration;
- no decorative element that competes with instructions.

## Visual and character direction

Preserve the approved Philoo identity:

- primary blues `#33BFED` and `#5BB8F5`;
- cream `#FBF8F3`;
- deep navy text;
- soft rounded display typography;
- layered card edges that appear pressable;
- cartoonish but not infantile proportions;
- restrained pastel secondary colors;
- no dark corporate lesson interface;
- no synthetic collage used merely to fill space.

Platão remains the canonical character from the approved reference. New poses
must preserve:

- face;
- hair and beard construction;
- eye color and scale;
- body height and proportions;
- purple garment and gold trim;
- sandals;
- rendering style.

Each pose is derived from the dialogue's physical intent and composition. If
Platão refers to an element on the left, his gaze and gesture orient left.
Reusable poses are allowed only where the action genuinely matches.

Required new pose intents:

- celebrating shadow recognition;
- interrupting with a curious question;
- revealing evidence behind the prisoners;
- demonstrating a causal sequence;
- introducing dóxa thoughtfully;
- modelling classification;
- reacting gently to revision;
- inviting the learner to turn.

Image panels are used only when the image communicates story evidence. Their
dimensions are reserved before loading to prevent layout shifts.

## Story transitions

Transitions support continuity:

- content exits with a short opacity and directional translation;
- the destination enters from the corresponding direction;
- the journey rail updates in synchrony with content arrival;
- duration remains restrained;
- reduced-motion mode uses a near-instant crossfade;
- no layout property such as height or width is animated.

The lesson must not show blank oversized cards during navigation.

## Known transient layout defect

Observed behavior:

- after client-side navigation into the current activity, the main guidance
  and status cards occasionally stretch vertically;
- Platão and copy become anchored near the bottom;
- refreshing restores the correct layout.

Treat this as a release-blocking defect for Lesson One.

Investigation requirements:

1. reproduce through rapid client navigation, normal navigation, back/forward,
   and restored journey state;
2. inspect container sizing during entry transitions;
3. ensure every flex/grid ancestor has a deterministic available height;
4. prevent stale animation transforms or measured dimensions from surviving a
   route change;
5. reserve image geometry before asset decode;
6. verify development and production builds;
7. add a regression test at the closest reliable layer;
8. visually repeat the transition multiple times at desktop and tablet sizes.

No speculative fix is accepted without reproduction evidence.

## Responsive behavior

### Desktop

- journey rail and main lesson surface coexist without overlap;
- no page scroll at the approved target viewport;
- activities use the available width without becoming dense;
- instructional dialog remains comfortably sized.

### Tablet

- the collapsed rail never overlaps or exceeds the lesson surface;
- story and activity cards remain readable in landscape and portrait;
- drag remains available where pointer/touch geometry is reliable;
- tap placement is always an equivalent option.

### Phone

- journey rail becomes a compact control;
- narrative content scrolls normally when required;
- activities switch to sequential decision layouts rather than shrinking a
  desktop board;
- Platão remains large enough to read emotionally;
- no instruction depends only on hover, drag, or tiny text.

## Teacher and independent-use value

The learner experience remains complete without a teacher. Later platform work
may expose:

- completion;
- first attempt and revision;
- common misconception categories;
- optional reflection responses;
- resume position.

This lesson does not add grades or punitive scoring. Teacher-facing reporting
must distinguish participation from demonstrated understanding.

## Acceptance criteria

The lesson is ready for template review when:

- the complete journey lasts approximately 12–15 minutes in a natural trial;
- every activity is preceded by clear instructions and can reopen them;
- no activity asks about content not yet taught;
- Platão provides contextual, non-punitive feedback;
- dóxa is accurately introduced and applied;
- the final scene creates desire to begin Lesson Two;
- all nine journey stages are visible and completed navigation remains
  revisit-able;
- desktop, tablet, and phone layouts are intentionally different where needed;
- keyboard, touch, reduced-motion, and modal focus behavior work;
- the transient oversized-card navigation defect is reproduced and resolved;
- focused tests, full tests, lint, and production build pass;
- all approved assets and code are committed and pushed to the Philoo
  repository.

## Out of scope

- Lesson Two implementation;
- Lesson Three implementation;
- teacher dashboard and analytics UI;
- grades, leaderboards, points, lives, or streak systems;
- sound design;
- virtual or augmented reality;
- a permanent activity-template gallery before Lesson One validation.
