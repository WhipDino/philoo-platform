# Philoo Dialogue Visual Language Design

## Goal

Make spoken dialogue immediately distinguishable from narration, tips, and
activity instructions without relying on explanatory labels such as “Platão
diz”.

## Visual language

- Philosopher dialogue uses a compact cream voice card, a prominent baby-blue
  opening quotation mark, conversational typography, and the philosopher's
  name as a quiet signature.
- Narration and introductions do not use quotation marks or the voice-card
  silhouette.
- Tips use a smaller baby-blue note treatment.
- Activity instructions use a purpose-built action panel with visible controls.

## Scene 3 experiment

Only the dialogue treatment in Scene 3 changes. The cave artwork, story copy,
three dialogue beats, progress, routes, and transitions remain unchanged.

The desktop voice card becomes narrower and taller so it remains in the dark
rock at the left of the composition and does not cover the distant prisoners.
Its corners are evenly rounded, with no speech tail or triangle. A large
opening quotation mark provides the spoken-dialogue signature. The `Platão`
signature remains subtle rather than becoming a sentence such as “Platão diz”.

On mobile, the card remains within the illustration's quiet upper region and
uses reduced padding and type sizes so the longest dialogue beat remains
readable without covering Platão.

## Success criteria

- The card is visibly spoken dialogue before its text is read.
- The dialogue is easier to scan because its lines are shorter.
- Platão, the cave path, and the three distant people remain visible.
- No new label, triangle, tail, illustration, dependency, or route is added.
